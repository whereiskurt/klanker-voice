The teardown playbook

Four lifecycle tiers, and the ordered procedure for reaching the one worth stopping at. Three gates can halt you; the first is the verification that never ran during development, and it is not optional.

StateAWSRound tripWhat it costs you
Running ~$190/mo — Full stack, 3.5 vCPU / 7 GB across voice, auth and telephony-edge.
kv pause ~$60/mo ~5 min · 1 approval Tasks at zero, but NAT Gateway and ALB keep burning ~$48/mo against traffic that isn't coming.
kv hibernate ~$14/mo ~20–25 min · 2 approvals The one to stop at. NAT, ALB and services destroyed; EIP, DNS, certs, DynamoDB, ledger, ECR all kept. No restore needed to return.
kv destroy ~$1/mo hours + restore Saves only ~$13/mo more and costs DNS, certs, regenerated bucket names, the NAT EIP, and a full restore. Not built yet.

The gap that matters is paused → hibernated: $46/mo, and you still come back in twenty-five minutes with nothing to restore.

If a gate fails

What each stop means

  • Gate 1, not "No changes" — the refactor touched your running stack. Stop and investigate; do not proceed.
  • Gate 1, EIP in the destroy list — retain_eip isn't taking effect. Hibernating would release the VoIP.ms-allowlisted address.
  • Gate 2 fails — services, ALB or NAT survived, or the EIP is gone. Fix the cause and re-run kv hibernate; the flag is already committed, so it re-attempts the remaining steps.
  • Phase 1 fails — later phases were never dispatched. kv pause status shows what actually applied.

While hibernated

What your users see

  • voice.klankermaker.ai serves a maintenance page and still unfurls correctly when the link is shared.
  • auth.klankermaker.ai is ALB-only — it does not resolve to a healthy origin at all.
  • All six DIDs stay provisioned and still billed; callers get a fast busy. A VoIP.ms-side failover recording would be kinder and costs nothing.
  • A CI build landing mid-hibernation will not clobber the maintenance page.

Don't forget

Outside AWS

  • ElevenLabs $22/mo survives every tier — and is now ~60% of your remaining bill. Kept deliberately for the cloned voice.
  • Check whether cancelling Creator preserves that cloned voice before treating it as safe to drop.
  • The DIDs are a separate line item in VoIP.ms billing, unchanged by any tier.
  • Measure the real floor in Cost Explorer after the first hibernation — the ~$10 residual is inferred, never measured.
